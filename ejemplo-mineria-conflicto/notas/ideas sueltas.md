# Loose notes — canon and conflict project

Notes from meetings and late nights. No order.

---

**March 12.** Conversation with J. about the question. She’s right: if I only
look at the number of conflicts per department vs. mining output, I’m not saying
anything Arellano‑Yanguas didn’t already say in 2011. The contribution should be
in the *content* of conflicts, not their count.

So: it’s not “where is there more conflict?” but “what are people fighting over,
and does that change with how much mining revenue arrives?”

---

**Problem with the canon.** The Finance Ministry has the district‑level transfer
data on a transparency portal, but it’s a 2000s webapp and there’s no way to export.
Tried for a while and gave up. Alternative: use MINEM’s mining output and employment
as a proxy — those do download cleanly.

Justification: the canon *is* 50% of corporate income tax from mining companies
distributed to producing areas. Output is a direct proxy, though imperfect
(international prices add noise, and there’s a one‑year+ lag from output to transfer).

**Write this explicitly in the paper.** Don’t hide it in a footnote.

---

**About the Ombudsman.** They’ve published 269 monthly reports since 2004 and none
are in Excel. All PDFs. Nobody has that series as a database.

Each case includes: type, start date, location (district/province/dept), narrative
description, primary/secondary/tertiary actors, and status.

If I can convert even a single year to CSV, I have something nobody else has. And
the narrative description is exactly what I need to classify what each conflict is about.

Note: the reports publish their own tables with official totals. I can use them to
verify my extraction didn’t drop cases.

---

**Categories to classify the demand** (first attempt, will likely change):

- water (pollution, access, headwaters)
- land and territory (easements, resettlement, borders)
- local employment (hiring local labor)
- revenue and compensation (canon, voluntary contributions, works‑for‑taxes)
- prior consultation and procedures
- breach of previous agreements

The last category interests me a lot. If many conflicts are about breached
agreements, then the issue isn’t how much money arrives but whether the State
delivers. That connects to the PCM commitments data.
